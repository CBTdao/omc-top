Salesforce9.0

The enterprise default — and in September 2026 it renamed its lineup. On 2026-09-03 Salesforce replaced the Enterprise ($175/user/month), Unlimited ($350) and Agentforce 1 editions with Core at $195, Advanced at $395 and Max at $550 per user/month billed annually — an effective 11.4% and 12.9% list-price lift on the two mid tiers, while Max holds Agentforce 1's $550. Below the top tier, AI is an add-on: Agentforce for Sales starts at $125/user/month, or you meter actions with Flex Credits at $500 per 100,000 credits (each action draws 20 credits, about $0.10; voice actions 30). Max bundles 2.75M Flex Credits per org per year. The entry tiers stay accessible: Starter Suite at $25 (the only edition billable monthly) and Pro Suite at $100. Licensing analysts read the restructure as a price increase in bundle clothing; Salesforce positions it as 70% more value. Both things are visible on the pricing page.

Pricing: Free Suite (2 users) · Starter $25 · Pro Suite $100 · Core $195 · Advanced $395 · Max $550 /user/mo, billed annually (except Starter); Agentforce for Sales add-on from $125/user/mo · Flex Credits $500 per 100k ($0.10/action)Best for: Enterprises that need the deepest customization and can price AI consumption separately

HubSpot8.9

The SMB platform play, now with a three-layer bill. The official Sales Hub page shows Starter starting at $7/mo/seat on annual billing — but that is a new-customer promotion against a $20 standard rate (monthly promo is $10), and the page does not state the renewal rate; one pricing analyst calls the $7 figure "the single most misreported fact about HubSpot pricing right now." Professional is $90/mo/seat annual ($100 monthly) plus a mandatory one-time $1,500 onboarding fee; Enterprise is $150 plus $3,500 onboarding. The AI lives in HubSpot Credits: every paid tier gets a monthly allowance (500 / 3,000 / 5,000) that resets and does not carry over, extras cost $9.00 per 1,000 credits, and the agents draw down on it — the Prospecting Agent consumes 100 credits per recommended outreach, the Customer Agent 50 per resolved conversation. Free CRM covers up to 2 users with no credit card.

Pricing: Free (2 users) · Starter $7/mo/seat promo ($20 standard) · Professional $90/mo/seat annual + $1,500 onboarding · Enterprise $150 + $3,500 onboarding · extra credits $9/1,000Best for: SMB and mid-market teams that want one GTM platform with AI baked in

Zoho CRM8.6

The value pick — and the outlier on AI metering. Zoho's official comparison page lists four paid editions at $14 (Standard), $23 (Professional), $40 (Enterprise) and $52 (Ultimate) per user/month billed annually, or $20/$35/$50/$65 billed monthly, with a genuinely usable free edition for 3 users. It is also the only top-five vendor that still bundles AI into the seat price instead of metering it separately: the official edition list puts AI agents and predictive intelligence in Professional at $23, and the Zia assistant plus AI anomaly detection in Enterprise at $40 — no credit wallet, no action surcharge. The trade-offs are product depth (the UI feels dated, and the AI trails Salesforce and HubSpot in autonomy) and per-organization fees that change at scale. For a budget-conscious team that wants AI features inside a predictable per-seat bill, it is the cheapest serious entry on this list.

Pricing: Free (3 users) · Standard $14 · Professional $23 · Enterprise $40 · Ultimate $52 /user/mo annual ($20/$35/$50/$65 monthly) · AI agents from Professional, Zia from Enterprise — bundled, not meteredBest for: Budget-conscious teams that want AI inside a flat per-seat price

Attio8.5

The AI-native upstart, priced with two meters at once: a workspace fee plus a per-seat fee. The official pricing page (rendered 2026-11-22) lists Plus at $50/month for a workspace with one user included, plus $44 for each added user on monthly billing — or $40 + $35 on annual billing, a 20% discount — capped at 10 seats; Pro is $125/$100 + $99/$79 with no seat cap; Ultra is $1,000/month for five users plus $189/seat, annual only. AI and enrichment draw from two credit pools: seat credits (500/user/month on Plus, 1,000 on Pro) and workspace credits (1,500/10,000), with top-ups from $70/month for 5,000 credits. Note the recency: Attio raised Plus from $36 to $44/month on 2026-08-12 without a press release, and major review aggregators still list $29 — the vendor page is the only live number. Pro gates the genuinely agentic features: call intelligence, email sequences and custom objects.

Pricing: Free (3 seats) · Plus $50 workspace + $44/seat monthly ($40 + $35 annual, ≤10 seats) · Pro $125 + $99 ($100 + $79 annual) · Ultra $1,000 (5 users) + $189/seat annual only · credit top-ups from $70/moBest for: Startups and modern GTM teams that want a flexible data model built AI-first

Pipedrive8.3

The sales-team CRM that kept AI inside the cheap tiers. The official pricing page lists four editions per seat/month billed annually: Lite at $14, Growth at $39, Premium at $59 and Ultimate at $79 — and, unusually, the entry tier is where AI lives: Lite ($14) includes AI-powered report creation and Pipedrive Nova, the built-in meeting-intelligence tool that briefs before meetings and pushes ready-to-approve CRM updates afterward. Premium ($59) adds the AI-powered multi-email tools (write, summarize and reply), custom scoring and enrichment. The lineup was recently renamed from Essential/Advanced/Professional/Power/Enterprise — any guide still citing those names, or a $49 Professional tier, describes an older price structure. No free tier (14-day trial only), and per-seat pricing is VAT-exclusive. For small sales teams that want pipeline-first software with AI included rather than metered, it is the friendliest bill on this list.

Pricing: Lite $14 · Growth $39 · Premium $59 · Ultimate $79 /seat/mo billed annually · AI reports + Nova meeting intelligence in Lite; AI email tools in Premium · no free tierBest for: Sales-first teams that want simple pipelines with AI included at the entry price

What the comparison tables hide

Same category, four different meters

Salesforce and HubSpot price AI agents as per-action credits on top of seats ($0.10 per Agentforce action; 100 HubSpot credits per prospecting outreach); Attio stacks three meters — workspace fee, seat fee and twin credit pools; Zoho and Pipedrive still bundle AI into the seat price ($23 Professional with AI agents; $14 Lite with AI reports and meeting intelligence). The seat price you compare across review sites is only the entry ticket. Before signing, price the meter: a five-rep Salesforce team running heavy agent workflows can spend more in Flex Credits than in seats, and a HubSpot Professional seat carries a mandatory $1,500 onboarding fee the sticker never mentions.

September 2026: Salesforce renamed its price increase

On 2026-09-03 Salesforce retired the Enterprise and Unlimited editions and introduced Core at $195 and Advanced at $395 — the same tiers as the old $175 Enterprise and $350 Unlimited, up 11.4% and 12.9%, while Max kept Agentforce 1's $550 exactly. The official framing is "70% more value than legacy Enterprise"; procurement analysts' framing is a list-price lift in bundle clothing, softened by bundled Slack, Tableau Next and Premier Success. Existing contracts keep legacy pricing until renewal. Most comparison sites still list Essentials, Enterprise and Unlimited — if a Salesforce quote uses names that no longer appear on the pricing page, you are being quoted a legacy SKU.

$7 is a promotion, not a rate

HubSpot's Starter card shows $7/mo/seat on annual billing — with $20 struck through beside it and a new-customers-only banner. That makes $7 the acquisition price and $20 the planning number: the page does not state the renewal rate, and promotional pricing is by definition not the renewal rate. The same pattern hides at Professional and Enterprise, where a mandatory one-time onboarding fee ($1,500 and $3,500) is charged automatically at checkout. When a CRM's headline price is a promo, budget on the struck-through number and ask the vendor what happens at renewal.

Aggregators lag; the vendor page is the only live number

Three of the five vendors on this list changed their lineup in 2026 without the review aggregators keeping up: Attio raised Plus from $36 to $44/month in August 2026 while G2 and Capterra still list $29; Pipedrive renamed its tiers (older guides cite Essential $14 / Advanced $27); Salesforce replaced Enterprise/Unlimited with Core/Advanced in September. Every stale page carries a recent "last updated" stamp — which refers to the page, not the price on it. For a category that reprices this fast, treat the vendor's own pricing page, fetched today, as the only source worth budgeting from.

FAQ

What does the AI in a CRM actually cost in 2026?

Depends entirely on the vendor's meter. Salesforce meters per action: Flex Credits cost $500 per 100,000 and each Agentforce action draws 20 credits (about $0.10), voice actions 30 — or the Agentforce for Sales add-on from $125/user/month. HubSpot includes a monthly credit allowance (500–5,000 by tier) that does not roll over, with extras at $9.00 per 1,000 credits; a Prospecting Agent outreach consumes 100 credits. Attio includes twin credit pools (per-user plus workspace) with top-ups from $70/month. Zoho and Pipedrive are the exceptions: their AI features are bundled into tier pricing with no usage meter. Multiply your expected agent activity by the credit price before comparing seat prices.

Which CRM includes AI in the seat price?

Zoho CRM and Pipedrive. Zoho's official edition list puts AI agents and predictive intelligence in Professional ($23/user/month annual) and the Zia assistant with AI anomaly detection in Enterprise ($40). Pipedrive puts AI-powered report creation and Nova meeting intelligence in Lite ($14/seat/month annual) and the AI email tools in Premium ($59). Everyone else meters: Salesforce sells AI as an add-on or per-action credits, HubSpot uses a monthly credit allowance, Attio uses per-user and workspace credit pools. If your workload is high-volume and you want a predictable bill, the two bundled vendors deserve a serious look despite lower model sophistication.

Is HubSpot Starter really $7 per month?

It is $7/mo/seat for new customers on annual billing — a limited-time promotion against the $20 standard rate, with a $10 monthly-billing promo variant. The pricing page does not state what you pay at renewal. Professional and Enterprise add mandatory one-time onboarding fees of $1,500 and $3,500 respectively. Plan your budget on the $20 standard rate, confirm renewal terms before signing, and note that the AI features consume credits: Starter includes only 500 credits per month, enough to try the Prospecting Agent but not to run it continuously.

The verdict

Buy by the meter, not the sticker. A sales-first team that wants AI included at the entry price: Pipedrive Lite at $14/seat — AI reports and meeting intelligence live in the cheapest tier. A budget-conscious team wanting AI in a flat seat price: Zoho Professional at $23 (Enterprise at $40 adds the Zia assistant). A startup that wants an AI-native data model: Attio Plus ($40 workspace + $35/seat annual, 10-seat cap) — and re-check the price before you sign, because it moved twice this year. An SMB standardizing on one platform: HubSpot Professional at $90 + $1,500 onboarding, budgeted on $20 Starter rates rather than the $7 promo. An enterprise already on Salesforce: the September 2026 Core/Advanced/Max relabel means every quote needs a fresh look — price the Flex Credits your agents will burn, not just the $195 seat. In this category the agent is the meter; the seat is only the ticket.
