Fathom8.9

The most generous free tier in the category, and the reason it ranks first: unlimited recordings, unlimited transcription and instant AI summaries at $0, with no monthly cap — against Otter's 300 minutes and Fireflies' 400-minute storage wall. It is built for sales teams: CRM field sync into HubSpot and Salesforce, coaching scorecards, and deal summaries that update records after the call. Bot-free capture (beta on Mac) records without a visible participant joining. Paid plans are per user with no credits, tokens or usage caps, and every paid tier carries a 90-day guarantee. The trade-off: coach-and-CRM depth comes on Team and Business, and SSO/SCIM sits on Enterprise only.

Pricing: Free (unlimited recordings & transcription, limited advanced summaries) · Premium $16/user/mo annual ($20 monthly) · Team $15/user/mo annual ($19 monthly, 2-user min) · Business $25 annual ($34 monthly) · Enterprise $35 annual-only (SSO/SCIM)Best for: Sales and customer-facing teams that want calls synced into the CRM
2

Otter.ai8.6

Still the best live meeting transcription and the best collaborative transcript editing in the category — several people can annotate the same running transcript while the call is happening, and the bot auto-joins Zoom, Meet and Teams. It now ships an MCP server so AI assistants can query your meeting history. The 2026 caveats are the meters: the free tier's 300 minutes/month comes with a 30-minute cap per conversation and 3 lifetime file imports, and — the single most common buying mistake here — Pro is not an unlimited-transcription plan: 1,200 minutes per user per month, 90 minutes per meeting, no rollover. Unlimited meetings start on Business.

Pricing: Basic free (300 min/mo, 30-min cap, 3 lifetime imports) · Pro $16.99/user/mo monthly ($8.33 annual), 1,200 min/mo · Business $30 monthly ($19.99 annual), unlimited · Enterprise customBest for: Live meetings with collaborative note-taking
3

Fireflies.ai8.5

The data-plumbing pick: built for piping meeting content into other systems, with integrations into Salesforce, HubSpot, Slack and Notion, an API on Business, and team-level conversation intelligence — talk-to-listen ratios, sentiment trends, topic tracking and deal signals. The free tier is genuinely unlimited on transcription and AI summaries, but storage is 400 minutes per team and nothing can be downloaded — two different meters, and only one of them is unlimited. Multi-language mode, team analytics and unlimited storage all sit on Business, not Pro: buying Pro expecting the sales-coaching features is the most common mistake here.

Pricing: Free (unlimited transcription, 400 min storage/team, no downloads) · Pro $10/seat/mo annual ($18 monthly), 8,000 min storage · Business $19 annual ($29 monthly), unlimited storage · Enterprise $39 annualBest for: Teams that want meeting data flowing into CRM and task tools
4

Zoom AI Companion8.3

Not a separate product — a feature of the conferencing platform, and that is its whole economics: AI Companion (meeting summaries, in-meeting Q&A, smart recordings, real-time captions) is included in every paid Zoom Workplace plan at no additional cost, so a team already paying for Zoom Pro ($14.16/user/mo) or Business ($18.33/user/mo) pays $0 extra. Zoom states it does not use customer content to train its AI models. The limits: capture is Zoom-native, so Meet and Teams meetings need the standalone AI Companion plan ($10/user/mo), and custom AI agents plus deep knowledge integrations sit behind the Custom AI Companion add-on ($12/user/mo). Before buying any standalone tool, price what you already own.

Pricing: Included in paid Zoom Workplace plans ($0 extra) · Standalone plan $10/user/mo · Custom AI Companion add-on $12/user/moBest for: Teams already on paid Zoom whose meetings happen on Zoom
5

Granola7.9

The bot-free notepad: it captures your computer's audio directly, so nothing joins the call and other participants never see an AI in the meeting — the deciding feature for client calls, investor meetings and anyone whose guests dislike recording bots. Its output is notes, not transcripts: no speaker labels, no audio or video recording, which shrinks the data footprint but also means verbatim quotes and accountability are not what it sells. In 2026 it repriced and collapsed its ladder: Basic is free for 25 meetings lifetime, and the former $18/month Individual plan was retired — Granola's own pricing FAQ now says reviews quoting an $18 individual plan do not match its current page. Teams now pay $14 per user, less than individuals used to.

Pricing: Basic free (25 meetings lifetime) · Business $14/user/mo · Enterprise $35/user/mo (SSO, SCIM, HIPAA workspaces)Best for: Individuals in back-to-back meetings who want notes without a bot

What the comparison tables hide

The bundle set the category's reference price at $0

Zoom bundles AI Companion into every paid Workplace plan, so for a Zoom Pro or Business user the marginal cost of meeting AI is nothing — while Microsoft charges $30 per user per month for Copilot on top of Teams. Every standalone tool on this list is now priced against "already included." If all your meetings happen on one platform, test the built-in before you buy anything; the standalone tools win when your meetings span platforms, or when the workflow (CRM sync, coaching, cross-meeting search) goes beyond what the bundle does.

Bot vs bot-free is a consent question, not a feature question

A meeting bot joins the call as a visible participant and everyone knows a transcript is being made. Device-level capture (Granola, Fathom's bot-free mode) is invisible to everyone else in the meeting. That changes the social contract of the call — and possibly the legal one: several US states and jurisdictions require all-party consent to record, and an invisible recorder does not change that obligation. Granola transcribes without storing audio, which narrows the data footprint; it does not narrow your duty to tell people. This is not legal advice — check your jurisdiction and your employer's policy.

The free tiers are real, but each one meters a different thing

Three tools advertise "free" and none of them are capped the same way. Fathom caps advanced summaries, not recordings or transcription. Fireflies caps storage — 400 minutes per team, and when it fills you cannot view new meetings until you delete old ones. Otter caps minutes (300/month) and per-conversation length (30 minutes) and lifetime file imports (3). Read what is capped, not the word "free" — the cap that matches your workload is the one that will hurt.

Buy by where the notes have to go

Call notes into a CRM with coaching metrics → Fathom Team or Business, or Fireflies Business. Live collaborative transcripts during the call → Otter. Meetings already on paid Zoom and nowhere else → AI Companion first, $0. Personal bot-free notes → Granola. The tools look interchangeable in a comparison table; in a workflow they are not, and the wrong match costs more than a few dollars a month.

FAQ

Is Zoom AI Companion really free?

It is included in paid Zoom Workplace plans at no additional cost — meeting summaries, in-meeting Q&A, smart recordings and real-time captions. It is not available on Zoom's free Basic plan. Two things do cost money: a standalone AI Companion plan at $10 per user per month for people who need summaries of non-Zoom meetings, and the Custom AI Companion add-on at $12 per user per month, which unlocks custom AI agents and deeper integrations. Zoom also states it does not use customer content to train its AI or third-party models.

Which AI meeting tool has the best free plan?

Depends on what you need uncapped. Fathom's free plan is the broadest: unlimited recordings, unlimited transcription, instant summaries — with advanced summaries limited to a handful per month. Fireflies' free plan gives unlimited transcription and summaries but 400 minutes of storage per team and no downloads. Otter's free plan gives 300 transcription minutes a month with a 30-minute cap per conversation and 3 lifetime file imports. Granola's free plan is 25 meetings, lifetime. If your meetings are long, Otter's 30-minute cap breaks first; if your archive is big, Fireflies' storage wall breaks first.

Do AI meeting tools comply with call-recording consent laws?

The tool does not decide that — you do. A visible bot joining the call is itself a form of disclosure, but several US states (California, Florida and others) and many jurisdictions require all-party consent to record a conversation, and device-level bot-free capture does not remove that obligation. Practical practice: announce recording at the start of the call, note it in the calendar invite, and check local law and employer policy before enabling silent capture. This site does not provide legal advice.

The verdict

In 2026 the category's reference price became $0 — Zoom's bundle made meeting AI "free" for anyone already paying for the platform, and every standalone tool now has to justify itself against that. Pick by workflow, not by feature list: sales teams that live in a CRM get the most from Fathom Team or Business; live collaborative transcripts are still Otter's game, but read the meters — Pro's 1,200 minutes and 90-minute meeting cap are the category's most-misread limit; meeting data flowing into other systems is Fireflies Business, not Pro; a team already on paid Zoom should turn on AI Companion before buying anything; and if the word "bot" is the problem, Granola is the quiet default, now cheaper for teams than it ever was for individuals. Whatever you pick, read what the free plan meters — recordings, storage, minutes or summaries — because that cap, not the sticker price, is the real product.
