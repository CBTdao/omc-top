The 30-second answer

The category splits on one question: does the deck have to come back out as a real .pptx? If you present inside the tool and only need a link, Gamma is the best-looking and fastest. If a client or an IT policy demands an editable PowerPoint file, only Canva and Plus AI do that cleanly. Beautiful.ai gives you enforced design consistency; Microsoft 365 Copilot only makes sense if you already pay for Microsoft 365. And Tome — still on many "best of" lists — shut its presentation product down in April 2025, so ignore every Tome price you read.

1

Gamma9.0

The best-looking AI-native decks, generated from a prompt or a pasted doc in under a minute. Card-based layout means it reflows like a web page rather than a slide grid — great for sharing a link, mixed for pixel-perfect control.

Pricing: Free (400 credits ≈ 10 decks) · Plus $8/mo annual ($10 monthly)Best for: Pitch decks, internal updates, anything you share as a link
2

Canva Magic Design8.8

The strongest free option by a wide margin, and one of only two here that exports a genuinely editable .pptx. You are trading AI-first generation for a mature, enormous template and brand-kit library.

Pricing: Free (PPTX export available) · Pro $12.99–18/moBest for: Anyone who needs a clean .pptx, teams with brand templates
3

Beautiful.ai8.5

Smart Slides enforce the design rules for you — add an element and the layout re-balances itself, which is why non-designers ship consistently decent decks with it. The trade-off is control: you cannot freely position everything.

Pricing: No permanent free tier · Pro $12–14.50/mo · 14-day trial (card required)Best for: Non-designers, teams that need visual consistency
4

Plus AI8.3

An AI add-in that lives inside PowerPoint and Google Slides rather than replacing them. There is no export step because you never left the real file format — for a deck that has to be edited by other people, this is the least-friction path.

Pricing: Free (limited) · Plus $10/user/mo billed annuallyBest for: Consultants and teams who must deliver native .pptx / Slides
5

Microsoft 365 Copilot7.8

Generates and edits decks from your own files, meetings and mail inside PowerPoint. The output is native by definition, but it is the most expensive item here and only worth it if Microsoft 365 is already your stack.

Pricing: Add-on $21/user/mo annual ($18 first-year promo)Best for: Enterprises already on Microsoft 365

What the landing pages won't tell you

The vendor

"Export to PowerPoint" almost never means "looks the same in PowerPoint." AI-native decks are built from web layout primitives; the .pptx export flattens them into placeholders. If the deck will be edited by someone else, start in a real .pptx tool instead of exporting into one.

The listicles

Tome still appears near the top of half the "best AI presentation tools" articles. Its presentation product was discontinued on 30 April 2025 and undeported decks were deleted. Any Tome pricing you read is fiction — the pages were never taken down.

The pricing

Gamma's "credits" are not slides — one generation can burn several, and regenerating burns more. The free tier looks generous until you iterate twice. Canva's free tier is the only genuinely usable one in this list.

The verdict

Start from your output requirement, not the tool. If you present live or share a link and nobody will unzip your file, Gamma wins on looks and speed for $8/month. If a real .pptx has to come out the other end, choose Canva (cheaper, huge template library) or Plus AI (stays inside PowerPoint, no export step). If you are not a designer and consistency matters more than freedom, Beautiful.ai will make you look more competent than you are. Microsoft 365 Copilot is the right answer only if it is already on the invoice. And it should go without saying, but: skip Tome.

Straight answers

Can I get a free AI presentation tool that exports .pptx?

Yes — Canva. Its free tier generates decks with Magic Design and lets you download them as editable .pptx, which none of the AI-native tools offer for free. The limitation is the AI is a co-pilot inside a design tool, not a one-prompt deck generator.

Why isn't Tome on this list?

Because Tome discontinued its presentation product on 30 April 2025; users were told to export their decks before that date and undeported ones were removed. Tome pivoted to a different product. Every "Tome pricing" page you find online predates the shutdown.

Does the .pptx export actually look right?

Rarely identical. Expect to fix spacing, fonts and image crops after exporting from any AI-native tool. If the deck must be editable by a client, generate it directly in PowerPoint or Google Slides using Plus AI, or accept a manual cleanup pass.

Is Microsoft 365 Copilot worth $21/user/month for slides?

Only if you are already paying for Microsoft 365 and want deck generation grounded in your own files and meetings. As a standalone presentation tool it is the most expensive option here and does none of it dramatically better than Plus AI at half the price.
