Klaviyo8.8

The ecommerce CRM that bills the profile, not the inbox. Klaviyo's official pricing page lists the Email plan at $20 per month for 500 active profiles and 5,000 emails, and the recommended Marketing plan at $25 per month for the same 500-profile tier — with a first month for $1 under promotion terms Klaviyo updated on 2026-11-18, valid through 2026-12-31. The free plan carries 250 active profiles, 500 emails, and — this is the 2026 part — a $10 monthly Composer usage credit and a Customer Agent capped at 10 resolutions per month; past that, the AI agent becomes an add-on billed by the resolution. So the same vendor now meters three different things: the list (active profiles), the writer (Composer, a dollar-denominated usage wallet), and the support agent (resolutions, an outcome metric identical in shape to what support desks charge). For Shopify-class stores with real revenue per profile, the profile meter aligns the vendor's incentive with yours — growth costs money, and Klaviyo says so on the tin.

Pricing: Email $20/mo (500 active profiles, 5,000 emails) · Marketing $25/mo (first month $1 to 2026-12-31) · Free 250 profiles/500 emails · Composer $10 usage · Customer Agent 10 resolutions/mo, then add-onBest for: Ecommerce stores that measure revenue per profile, not per newsletter

Mailchimp8.4

The incumbent, priced by a list you do not fully control. Mailchimp's official pages list Standard at $20 per month for 500 contacts with a send limit of 12 times your contact count (6,000 emails), and Premium at $350 per month for 10,000 contacts at 15 times (150,000 emails). An independent price-timeline report documents Standard at $14.99 in 2022 — a 33% rise to $20 — and the free plan shrinking from 2,000 contacts (2022) to 500 (2023) to 250, with automation stripped from the free tier in mid-2025. The one AI that is free, Intuit Assist, is a beta available to Premium, Standard and Legacy plans in English only, "at no additional cost at this time" — a qualifier that has an expiry date built into it. The company context is the loudest signal in this category: on 2026-11-20 Intuit agreed to sell Mailchimp to Bending Spoons at an enterprise value of about $3.68 billion, against the roughly $12 billion Intuit paid in November 2021 — a 69% write-down on a business whose revenue has been flat since 2024. Buy the seat for what it does today; treat the roadmap as somebody else's turnaround project.

Pricing: Standard $20/mo (500 contacts, 6,000 sends = 12×) · Premium $350/mo (10,000 contacts, 150,000 sends = 15×) · Free 250 contacts · Intuit Assist free add-on (beta, paid plans, English)Best for: Teams already inside the Mailchimp ecosystem that value the template library and integrations

Constant Contact8.2

The transparent one, with the send arithmetic printed on the page. Constant Contact's own homepage FAQ lists Lite at $12, Standard at $35 and Premium at $80 per month as starting prices, and its official partner pricing sheet spells out the meter the homepage leaves implicit: the monthly email send limit is 10 times your contact count on Lite, 12 times on Standard, and 24 times on Premium, with sends over the allowance billed at $0.002 each. Prepaying buys 10% off for six months or 15% off for twelve. The AI copy generator ships on every plan including Lite; the AI campaign builder and its three automation templates arrive on Standard, and Premium unlocks unlimited automations. What makes this easy to recommend is that nothing is hidden: two official documents agree on every number, the overage rate is published rather than discovered on an invoice, and phone support is included on every plan. For a small business that wants predictable bills over maximal AI, it is the safest seat on this list.

Pricing: Lite $12 · Standard $35 · Premium $80 per month (starting) · send limit = contacts × 10/12/24 · overage $0.002/send · prepay −10%/−15% · AI copy generator on all plansBest for: Small businesses that want published overage math and phone support

Brevo8.1

The multichannel suite with the fastest-moving price list we tracked this month. Brevo's current official pricing page lists Starter at $17, Standard at $35 and Professional from $499 per month, with a free plan that sends up to 300 emails per day. Five days earlier, an archived capture of the same official page — timestamped 2026-11-22 — still showed Starter at $9 and Standard at $18, and the current page's live Black Friday banner (code BREVOFRIDAY2026) confirms both snapshots belong to the same vendor within one week. That is a near-doubling of list prices in days, and every aggregator still showing $9 is now quoting a dead number. The product itself earns its slot: AI content generation ships from Starter, AI send-time optimization from Standard, Aura AI segmentation and an AI data analyst from Professional, and — notable for 2026 — "MCP for ChatGPT & Claude" is listed as a Starter feature, meaning an AI assistant outside Brevo can manage campaigns and contacts inside it. Verify the price at checkout, not in a review.

Pricing: Starter $17 · Standard $35 · Professional from $499/mo (current list) · was $9/$18 on the 2026-11-22 official snapshot · free 300 emails/day · MCP for ChatGPT & Claude from StarterBest for: Teams that want email + SMS + WhatsApp in one suite and agents that can drive it

MailerLite8.0

The budget pick that ships agentic email on the cheap tier. MailerLite's official pricing page lists the Comfort plan at $12 per month ($10.80 billed yearly) with 5,000 monthly emails, 50 automations, and its HeyLite AI writing layer; Power at $25 ($22.50 yearly) removes the email cap entirely and adds unlimited seats; the free plan carries 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails per month. An archived capture of the same official page from 2026-11-21 shows identical numbers, so this is the steadiest price list on this page. The 2026 detail is in the fine print of the free tier: "MCP for Claude, ChatGPT, and more" is listed among free-plan core features, meaning MailerLite is the only tool here that lets an external AI agent manage a newsletter before you have paid anything. The honest caveat is scope — HeyLite drafts and adjusts copy, it does not run autonomous journeys, and the plan is priced for senders who list-size growth slower than the meter. For solo newsletters and small shops, that trade is usually the right one.

Pricing: Comfort $12/mo ($10.80 yearly), 5,000 emails · Power $25/mo ($22.50 yearly), unlimited emails · Free 250 subscribers/2,500 emails · MCP free · HeyLite AI from ComfortBest for: Solo newsletters and small shops that want the lowest stable price plus agent access

What the comparison tables hide

Every vendor counts a different "contact"

Klaviyo bills active profiles — contacts you can market to. Mailchimp bills everyone in the audience: per an independent price-timeline report, unsubscribed, non-subscribed and duplicate contacts count toward the plan limit, so a 5,000-subscriber list with 3,000 unsubscribes costs as if it were 8,000 unless you clean it. Brevo and MailerLite bill subscribers; Constant Contact bills contacts times a plan multiplier. Two platforms holding the same real audience can print different invoice sizes, and none of the aggregated "from $X" prices tells you which denominator was used. Before comparing tools, export your list, count it the way each vendor counts it, and price all five.

Send allowance: a multiple, a quota, or infinity

Three structures coexist in one category. Mailchimp (12× contacts on Standard, 15× on Premium) and Constant Contact (10×/12×/24×, then $0.002 per extra send) tie sends to list size — a bigger bill for growth, printed as a ratio. Klaviyo and Brevo sell a fixed monthly quota (5,000 emails at the entry tier) — a ceiling you outgrow by sending, not by listing. MailerLite's Power plan removes the cap entirely at $25. The same campaign calendar can fit all three models at 500 contacts and blow up only one of them at 50,000, so the question is not "how many emails do I send" but "what happens when I send more than the tier allows".

AI is becoming the second meter

Klaviyo splits its AI into two meters: Composer, a $10-a-month usage wallet, and Customer Agent, priced per resolution — the same outcome metric support desks charge by, and the reason per-resolution pricing has now reached email. Mailchimp keeps Intuit Assist free but gates it to paid plans and a beta tag. Brevo gates AI features by tier (copy from Starter, send-time from Standard, segmentation from Professional). MailerLite bundles HeyLite into a $12 plan and gives away the MCP integration. Five vendors, four different answers to "is AI included": wallet, outcome, free add-on, tier gate, bundle. Read each card's AI row the way you would read a shipping table — it is where the next price increase lands first.

The category reprices faster than the aggregators

Within one week of November 2026, Brevo's official page moved Starter from $9 to $17 and Standard from $18 to $35 — our archived and live captures of the same vendor differ by five days — and Intuit agreed to sell Mailchimp at roughly a 69% discount to its 2021 purchase price. Mailchimp's Standard plan rose from $14.99 (2022) to $20 (2026) per an independent timeline. The pattern is one direction: entry floors rise, free tiers shrink (Mailchimp's free list went 2,000 → 500 → 250), and review sites quote last quarter's numbers. Any "cheapest email marketing tool" table built from cached prices is a table of expired coupons.

FAQ

How much does AI email marketing cost in 2026?

Entry prices on this list run from $12 to $25 per month at the smallest list tier: MailerLite Comfort $12 (5,000 emails), Constant Contact Lite $12, Mailchimp Standard $20 (500 contacts), Klaviyo Email $20 (500 profiles), Klaviyo Marketing $25, Brevo Starter $17. The AI features arrive at every level, but under different meters — a usage wallet at Klaviyo, a tier gate at Brevo, a free beta add-on at Mailchimp, a bundle at MailerLite. The number to compute before choosing is your list size counted the way each vendor counts it, because the contact meter, not the AI, drives the invoice.

What does Klaviyo cost for 500 contacts?

Klaviyo's official pricing page lists the Email plan at $20 per month for 500 active profiles with 5,000 emails, and the recommended Marketing plan at $25 for the same tier — with the first month at $1 under promotion terms valid through 2026-12-31 and updated on 2026-11-18. The free plan covers 250 active profiles and 500 emails. Above 500 profiles the price steps up by tier, and AI beyond the free allowances (Composer past $10 of usage, Customer Agent past 10 resolutions per month) is billed as an add-on.

Why did Intuit sell Mailchimp?

On 2026-11-20 Intuit announced an agreement to sell Mailchimp to Bending Spoons at an enterprise value of about $3.68 billion — $3.31 billion in cash plus $368 million in Bending Spoons stock — five years after buying it for roughly $12 billion in November 2021. The price cut by about 69% tracks the business: Mailchimp revenue was $1.3 billion in the fiscal year ended July 2026, flat since 2024, and fell 1% year over year to $313 million in Intuit's first fiscal quarter of 2027. Intuit said the sale lets it focus on QuickBooks, TurboTax and Credit Karma; the deal is expected to close in early 2027 pending regulatory approval.

Are the AI features included in the plan or billed separately?

Depends on the vendor, and the difference is money. Klaviyo bills Composer as usage ($10 of credits included monthly) and Customer Agent per resolution (10 free per month). Mailchimp includes Intuit Assist at no additional cost, but only on paid plans, only in English, and only in beta. Brevo gates features by tier: AI copy from Starter ($17), send-time optimization from Standard ($35), Aura segmentation and the AI data analyst from Professional ($499). MailerLite bundles HeyLite into Comfort ($12) and lists MCP for Claude and ChatGPT even on the free plan. Constant Contact puts the AI copy generator on every plan and the campaign builder from Standard ($35). Always read the AI row of the pricing table — it is where the next increase shows up first.

The verdict

Pick by list economics, then by AI meter. An ecommerce store measuring revenue per profile: Klaviyo, $20 to $25 at 500 profiles — and budget the Customer Agent resolutions separately, because that is the meter support desks taught the category. A small business that wants published overage math and a phone number: Constant Contact at $12–$35, where sends are contacts times a printed multiplier. A solo newsletter: MailerLite Comfort at $12, the steadiest price on the page with agent access in the free tier. A team that wants one suite for email, SMS and WhatsApp: Brevo — but price it at checkout, because its list prices moved twice this month. An organization already inside Mailchimp: stay for the templates, audit your contact count monthly, and read the acquisition news before renewing anything annual. In every case the same test applies: count your list the way each vendor counts it, then ask what the AI costs past its free allowance.
