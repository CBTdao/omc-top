Microsoft 365 Copilot8.9

The Excel option that is not sold as a spreadsheet product. Microsoft's official page lists Copilot at $30 per user per month paid yearly, or $31.50 paid monthly on an annual commitment — and a separate licence for a qualifying Microsoft 365 plan is required, so $30 is an add-on, not a price. What it does in Excel, in Microsoft's own words: analyse data, create charts and generate insights from natural-language prompts, inside the file you already have. Two things the sticker hides. First, the same brand sells a second, similarly named SKU at a different price — Copilot Business for Microsoft 365 Business Standard and Premium customers is $21 per user per month, with an $18 introductory promotion that ran through 2026-09-30 — so a buyer comparing "Copilot" across pages may be comparing two products. Second, because Copilot cannot be bought standalone, every per-seat total must include the base plan; an independent pricing tracker puts the all-in E3-with-Copilot seat near $69 a month and E5 near $90, which is the number a budget owner should see. For a shop already standardised on Excel and SharePoint, it is still the shortest path to AI inside a spreadsheet: nothing else on this list can read your tenant's files.

Pricing: $30/user/mo paid yearly · $31.50 paid monthly (annual commitment) · requires a qualifying Microsoft 365 licence · SMB Copilot Business $21 (promo $18 to 2026-09-30)Best for: Excel-and-SharePoint shops that want AI inside the file, not in another tab
2

Google Sheets with Gemini8.7

The default answer for most people, with a tier trap. Google's official Workspace pricing lists Starter at $7, Standard at $14 and Plus at $22 per user per month on the annual plan (flexible billing is $8.40 / $16.80 / $26.40), and Google's own AI page marks Gemini in Sheets as available on Standard and above — on Starter you get Gemini in Gmail and nothing in Sheets. So spreadsheet AI has no separate price tag; it arrives with a $7-per-seat tier upgrade that also buys 2 TB of pooled storage and Gemini in Docs, Slides and Meet. Two Google documents agree on both facts, which matters because Workspace pricing is the most region-sensitive number on this list. What you get for the money is auditability: Gemini writes a formula into a real cell, so a wrong answer looks like a wrong formula you can click on, not a confident paragraph in a side panel. Check the tier before you trial it — the cheapest Workspace plan will make the feature look broken rather than gated.

Pricing: Starter $7 · Standard $14 · Plus $22 /user/mo annual (flexible $8.40/$16.80/$26.40) · Gemini in Sheets from Standard, absent on StarterBest for: Teams already living in Google Workspace that want auditable formulas in cells
3

Quadratic8.4

The analyst's spreadsheet: one grid that runs formulas, Python, SQL and JavaScript, with an AI layer that writes and debugs the code. The pricing page is where the design shows. Personal is free with "limited AI usage"; Pro is $18 per user per month billed annually and includes $20 of AI credits; Business is $36 and includes $40 of credits. Read that as a bundle rather than a seat: at $18 for $20 of credits, the seat is priced below the credits it carries, so the number that governs your bill is how fast the model burns credits, not how many seats you buy — a four-person Pro team pays $72 for $80 of credit. The trade is the file format. Quadratic is its own canvas, and anything that depends on a spreadsheet being a spreadsheet file — finance templates, auditor workflows, a colleague's macro — does not come with you. For analysis-heavy work with real code underneath, nothing else here is as honest about what the AI is doing, because you can read the Python it wrote.

Pricing: Personal free (limited AI) · Pro $18/user/mo annual incl. $20 AI credits · Business $36 incl. $40 credits · Enterprise customBest for: Analysts who want Python and SQL in the grid, not a chat window
4

Rows8.2

The AI-data-analyst spreadsheet, metered in AI tasks — and the vendor's own plan cards now carry an acquisition banner. Official pricing: Free at $0 with 5 AI tasks a month; Plus at $6 per user per month billed annually ($8 monthly) with 200 tasks, daily data-table automation and a 5 MB import cap; Pro at $59 per month plus $6 per user annually ($79 + $8 monthly) with 1,000 tasks, per-minute automation and 100 MB imports. The meter is the point: at Plus an AI task costs $0.03, while Pro with a single seat costs $65 for 1,000 tasks, or $0.065 each — upgrading buys five times the capacity at more than twice the unit price, because the platform fee scales with the allowance rather than discounting it. The strategic question is printed on every plan card: "Rows has joined Superhuman." A pricing page that announces its own acquisition is telling you the roadmap is under new management; buy the seat for what it does today, and treat the 2027 feature list as a bet rather than a plan.

Pricing: Free (5 AI tasks/mo) · Plus $6/user/mo annual ($8 monthly), 200 tasks · Pro $59/mo + $6/user annual ($79 + $8 monthly), 1,000 tasksBest for: Teams that want an AI analyst over live business data in a standalone grid
5

Coefficient8.1

The connector, not the spreadsheet: Coefficient keeps Excel and Google Sheets and fills them with live data from Salesforce, HubSpot, warehouses and SQL, with AI functions running on the sheet itself. Official pricing: Free at $0 with three standard data sources, a 5,000-row import cap and 50 refreshes a month; Starter at $49 per month for one account with 500 refreshes and daily auto-refresh; Pro at $99 per user per month (billed per user, capped at five users) with unlimited rows, 5,000 refreshes and hourly refresh. The pricing geometry is worth doing on paper: Starter's allowance belongs to one account, while Pro's allowance is shared by the team but priced per seat — one Pro seat is $99 for 5,000 refreshes ($0.0198 each), five Pro seats are $495 for the same 5,000 refreshes ($0.099 each), which lands back exactly where Starter started. There is no volume discount here; you are buying refresh frequency and row ceilings. If the problem is "the numbers in my sheet are three days old", this is the cheapest fix on the list. If the problem is "I cannot write the formula", it is the wrong tool.

Pricing: Free (3 sources, 5,000-row import, 50 refreshes/mo) · Starter $49/mo (1 account, 500 refreshes) · Pro $99/user/mo (max 5 users, unlimited rows, 5,000 refreshes)Best for: Teams whose spreadsheets need live CRM and warehouse data more than they need prose

What the comparison tables hide

Three meters, one category

Copilot is a seat add-on at $30 with a base licence underneath; Workspace sells spreadsheet AI as a $7 tier step (Starter $7 to Standard $14); Rows sells AI tasks (5 free, 200 at Plus, 1,000 at Pro); Quadratic sells a credit wallet ($20 of credits inside an $18 seat); Coefficient sells refreshes and row ceilings ($49 for one account, $99 per seat up to five). Nothing in that list is comparable as printed. The only common denominator is cost per 1,000 AI actions, and only Rows and Coefficient publish enough on their pricing pages to compute it — which is why a "cheapest AI spreadsheet" table built from sticker prices is a table of five different units.

The free tier is the trial, and it is one prompt long

Rows gives 5 AI tasks a month on Free. Coefficient's free plan caps imports at 5,000 rows with 50 refreshes. Quadratic's Personal plan says "limited AI usage" without a number. Workspace Starter excludes Gemini in Sheets entirely, so the cheapest Google plan cannot test the feature at all, and Copilot has no free tier — it requires a paid Microsoft 365 licence before the $30 even applies. In three of these five products the feature you are evaluating is not reachable on the free plan, which turns "try before you buy" into "buy to try". Ask for a trial of the paid tier, not the free one.

$30 does not buy Copilot

Microsoft's page is explicit: "A separate license for a qualifying Microsoft 365 plan is required." The $30 is therefore a surcharge on a seat you already rent, and the real per-seat figure is base plan plus $30. One independent tracker puts E3 with Copilot near $69 per seat per month and E5 near $90 after Microsoft's July 2026 base-suite increase — a number that never appears on the Copilot page because the Copilot page is not selling the stack. Meanwhile the same brand sells Copilot Business at $21 with an $18 promotion, for SMB plans. Same word on three pages, three numbers: read the SKU, not the logo.

The formula is the audit trail

Our view, stated as a view: the difference that matters between these tools is where the AI's output lands. Copilot, Gemini and Coefficient write into cells, so a wrong answer is a wrong formula you can inspect, and a right answer survives the person who asked for it. Rows and Quadratic answer first and write second, which is faster and harder to audit — Quadratic compensates by showing you the Python it ran, which is the only version of "show your work" that a spreadsheet can offer. When two tools cost the same per month, buy the one whose output your successor can check.

FAQ

How much do AI spreadsheet tools cost in 2026?

Between $0 and $99 per user per month, and the spread is mostly a difference in units rather than value. Bundled into an existing suite: Gemini in Sheets from $14 per user per month (Workspace Standard, annual), or Copilot at $30 on top of a Microsoft 365 licence. Standalone tools: Rows from $6 (200 AI tasks), Quadratic from $18 ($20 of credits included), Coefficient from $49 for a single account. The number to compute before comparing is cost per 1,000 AI actions at your expected volume — Rows Plus is $15 per 1,000 tasks, Pro is $65 per 1,000 at one seat.

Is Copilot in Excel included in Microsoft 365?

No. Microsoft lists Microsoft 365 Copilot at $30 per user per month paid yearly ($31.50 paid monthly on an annual commitment), and requires "a separate license for a qualifying Microsoft 365 plan" — the add-on cannot be bought standalone. Copilot Chat, the web-grounded chat with no access to your tenant data beyond what Copilot Chat itself can see, is free for Entra account holders with an eligible subscription; that is a different product from Copilot inside Excel.

Do I have to leave Excel or Google Sheets to get AI?

No, and for most teams you should not. Copilot, Gemini and Coefficient all work inside the file you already have, which keeps formulas, permissions, macros and auditor workflows intact. Quadratic and Rows are standalone canvases: they are better at code-heavy analysis and worse at being the file your finance team sends to its accountant. The migration cost is real and rarely appears in reviews — a spreadsheet tool is only as useful as the files it can open.

Which option is cheapest for occasional use?

For a Google Workspace shop, Standard at $14 per user per month is the cheapest plan that includes Gemini in Sheets — the $7 Starter plan does not, so it is not a cheaper version of the same thing. For a solo analyst outside both suites, Rows Plus at $6 (200 AI tasks) is the lowest paid entry here, with SheetAI's $12 hosted tier as a lighter alternative if you only need cell-level functions. Avoid paying for a Pro tier to test a workflow you have not yet defined.

The verdict

Buy by the meter, not by the logo. Already on Microsoft 365 and storing files in SharePoint: Copilot at $30 per seat — budget base plan plus add-on, not $30, and check whether the SMB Copilot Business SKU at $21 covers your plan. Already on Google Workspace: Standard at $14, because Starter at $7 does not include Gemini in Sheets at all. An analyst who wants Python and SQL in the grid: Quadratic at $18, and watch credit burn rather than seat count. A team that needs an AI analyst over live business data: Rows Plus at $6 — but read the acquisition banner and treat the roadmap as a bet. A team whose real problem is stale data inside Excel or Sheets: Coefficient at $49, where the meter is refreshes, not intelligence. In every case the same test applies: price 1,000 AI actions at your own volume, then ask which of these tools writes its answer somewhere you can check.
