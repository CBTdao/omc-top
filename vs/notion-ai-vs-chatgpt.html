The 30-second answer

They're not rivals. ChatGPT is a general assistant with no memory of your work; Notion AI is a writing tool that already knows everything in your workspace. If your work lives in Notion, Notion AI saves more time. If it doesn't, ChatGPT does more things.

What the landing pages won't tell you

Notion AI

The pitch is "AI that knows your workspace". The catch is that it's a $10 add-on on top of a $10–$20 plan — you're paying $20–$30 to end up in the same place as one $20 ChatGPT subscription.

ChatGPT

Its weakness isn't intelligence, it's amnesia. Every project starts with you re-explaining what you're working on, unless you build the habit of maintaining a project context.

Both

The real question isn't which is smarter — it's whether your work already lives in Notion. If it does, the context advantage beats the model advantage almost every time.

The verdict

If your documents, notes and projects already live in Notion, the AI add-on is the better buy — it removes context-switching and the daily copy-paste tax, and that convenience compounds. If you need a tool that also writes code, analyses data, generates images or browses the web, ChatGPT is the only one of the two that can. Plenty of people pay for both, and it's not irrational: Notion AI for the writing that stays in Notion, ChatGPT for everything else.

Straight answers

Do I need a paid Notion plan for AI?

Yes — Notion AI is an add-on on top of a workspace plan for individual and most team tiers. The exception is Notion Business, where AI is bundled. Check current Notion pricing, since bundling has shifted several times.

Can ChatGPT read my Notion pages?

Yes, if you connect Notion as a data source in ChatGPT's connectors and your plan supports it. That narrows the gap — but it's a manual set-up, and the two-way convenience of writing directly in Notion is still Notion AI's advantage.

Is Notion AI a worse model than ChatGPT?

Slightly, at the frontier — Notion abstracts away the model layer and doesn't always run the newest flagship. For document writing and summarising, the gap is barely noticeable. For hard reasoning or code, it's real.
