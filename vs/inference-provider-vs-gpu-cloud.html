Inference provider vs GPU cloud: buy tokens, or rent the GPUs behind them (2026)

An inference provider sells you tokens over an API — you pay per million, and pay nothing between requests. A GPU cloud rents you the accelerators themselves — you pay per GPU-hour, whether or not the card is busy. The same open-weights model usually sits on both rails, so the choice is not about quality; it is about your load shape. In November 2026 the two markets are both liquid enough to price precisely: DeepSeek-V3 output ranges from $0.20 to $1.50 per 1M tokens across 14 tracked hosts, and a dedicated H100 rents for $1.99–$6.16 per GPU-hour on the published rate cards of Together, DeepInfra and CoreWeave. All figures below are from the vendors' own pricing pages, checked 2026-11-15, cross-checked against independent price trackers.

The 30-second answer

Buy tokens by default; rent GPUs only past the break-even. The break-even is arithmetic, not opinion: a dedicated H100 at DeepInfra's own $2.90/GPU-hour beats its own $0.89-per-1M serverless output price only if you push more than 3.26 million output tokens per hour, sustained (about 904 output tokens/second). For a model it serves at $0.32-per-1M output, the bar rises to 9.06M tokens/hour. Spiky or low-volume traffic: serverless wins before you do any math. Steady, saturating, multi-tenant load: hourly starts winning — and the commitment ladder ($1.99 → $3.99 → $5.49 for the same H100 at Together) prices exactly who eats the idle risk.

What the landing pages won't tell you

Inference provider

The provider market is itself a market, and most buyers never shop it. One model — DeepSeek-V3 — has 14 tracked hosts, and the output-price spread runs from $0.20 (Hyperbolic) to $1.50 (Nebius) per 1M tokens: a 7.5× gap for identical weights. The same model costs $1.10 output on DeepSeek's own API and $0.89 on DeepInfra. Price the host, not the model.

GPU cloud

One GPU can carry three prices in one rate card. Together lists the same HGX H100 at $1.99 preemptible, $3.99 on-demand and $5.49 in its Dedicated Inference tier. The ladder is not about the silicon — it prices who eats the idle risk: preemptible means you accept eviction, dedicated means the host guarantees capacity and you fund the guarantee.

The break-even

Utilization decides, and the formula is one division (ours): hourly rate ÷ price per 1M output tokens = output tokens per hour you must sustain. At DeepInfra's own rates, $2.90 ÷ $0.89 = 3.26M tokens/hour (~904 output tokens/second) before a dedicated H100 beats its own serverless price. Against its $0.32 Llama-3.3-70B-Turbo output rate, the bar rises to 9.06M/hour (~2,517 tok/s). Run the division before you rent anything.

The verdict

In November 2026 the default is to buy tokens: the serverless market for open-weights models is liquid, priced to the hundredth of a dollar, and charges you nothing between requests. Rent a GPU only when your load is steady enough to survive the division: hourly rate ÷ output price per 1M tokens = the sustained tokens-per-hour bar. At DeepInfra's own published rates that bar is 3.26M output tokens per hour on DeepSeek-V3 and 9.06M on Llama-3.3-70B. If your forecast clears it with headroom, take the hourly route — and prefer the commitment tiers only when the workload is genuinely 24/7, because the $1.99–$5.49 ladder for the same H100 is the market charging you for certainty. Note what one card can hold: DeepSeek-V3's 671B parameters exceed a single H100's 80 GB even at 8-bit, so "dedicated DeepSeek-V3" means multi-GPU serving, not one card.

Straight answers

What is the difference between an inference provider and a GPU cloud?

An inference provider bills per token and serves a model it operates — you send an OpenAI-compatible request, it returns text, and between requests you pay nothing. A GPU cloud bills per GPU-hour and gives you the raw accelerators — you install the weights, run the serving stack, and the meter runs whether the card is busy or idle. On November 2026 rate cards, DeepSeek-V3 output costs $0.20–$1.50 per 1M tokens across 14 tracked hosts, while a dedicated H100 rents for $1.99–$6.16 per GPU-hour on Together, DeepInfra and CoreWeave's own price pages.

When is renting a GPU cheaper than buying tokens?

When your sustained output volume clears the break-even: hourly rate ÷ price per 1M output tokens = output tokens per hour. Using DeepInfra's own published numbers, its $2.90 H100-hour must carry more than 3.26M output tokens per hour (~904 tok/s, sustained) to beat its own $0.89-per-1M DeepSeek-V3 output price; against its $0.32 Llama-3.3-70B-Turbo rate the bar is 9.06M tokens/hour. Monthly, a $2.90 H100 costs $2,088 for 720 hours — the token-equivalent is about 2.35B output tokens per month at DeepSeek-V3 rates.

Can one H100 actually serve DeepSeek-V3?

No — and this matters for reading the break-even. DeepSeek-V3 has 671B parameters; at 8-bit precision the weights alone are roughly 671 GB, far beyond one H100's 80 GB of HBM. A dedicated DeepSeek-V3 deployment means multi-GPU serving, so the per-GPU break-even for that model is a sizing thought experiment. For a model that fits one card — Llama-3.3-70B at 8-bit is roughly 70 GB — the arithmetic is directly actionable, which is why the 70B break-even (9.06M tokens/hour at DeepInfra's rates) is the honest planning number.
